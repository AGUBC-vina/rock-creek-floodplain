## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document refines the stakeholder concept draft into a structured technical description with explicit assumptions and a parameter table. Where source documents in this repository (Nord Feasibility Study, Sand Creek / Rock Creek Reclamation District Infiltration Feasibility Study, Singer Creek HDP, Caltrans D3 memorandum) supply a value, the value is **cited to the source**. Where a value is genuinely absent from those sources, it is flagged `[VERIFY: DATA MISSING]`.

**Critical transferability discipline.** The Nord study examined a geometrically similar Rock-Creek-to-Sand-Creek diversion but relied on **dams** for storage and attenuation. This concept uses **many small rock weirs** distributed along overland corridors — fundamentally different storage, attenuation, sediment, and footprint behavior. Nord's hydrology, watershed delineation, soils data, and diversion-geometry envelope are transferable inputs to this concept. Nord's **dam-storage-volume attenuation results, dam-impact mitigation costs, dam-foundation geotechnical requirements, and reservoir-inundation environmental impacts are NOT transferable** and are flagged as such throughout.

## 2. Project Description

### 2.1 Diversion (Intake) Structure

A controlled diversion structure on Rock Creek redirects a portion of high flood flow northward only during high-flow conditions. Normal and low flows remain in the Rock Creek channel.

- **Location.** Approximately **1.1 miles upstream of the Rock Creek / Keefer Slough bifurcation** (per concept draft).
  - **Context from Nord.** Nord's Sand Creek Diversion Alternative placed the intake **~7,600 ft (~1.4 mi) upstream of the bifurcation**; Nord's Tributary Diversion Alternative placed it ~3,200 ft (~0.6 mi) upstream (*Nord_Feasibility_Study_Part_A.md*, Sec. 9.2.1 and 9.2.2). The concept's 1.1-mi location is bracketed by these two precedents.
- **Design intent.** Pass low and moderate flows downstream undisturbed; activate diversion at a triggering threshold; route the diverted excess to corridor heads.
- **Type / capacity / trigger.** `[VERIFY: DATA MISSING — design diversion capacity (cfs), triggering flow threshold (cfs), and structure type (weir + bypass channel vs. side-channel spillway vs. gated structure) require screening-level hydraulic analysis. See Deliverable 5 (Screening Spreadsheet Structure).]`
- **Reference envelopes from Nord (DAM-BASED PRECEDENT — for size context only, NOT direct transfer):**
  - Sand Creek Alt.: diversion structure 307 ft long × 8 ft high; flow area 240 sf; design diversion 4,955 cfs at Q100 (*Nord_Feasibility_Study_Part_A.md*, Table 6).
  - Tributary Alt.: diversion structure 300 ft long × 21 ft high; flow area 140 sf; design diversion 3,725 cfs at Q100 (*Nord_Feasibility_Study_Part_A.md*, Table 7).
  - **Transferability note.** Both Nord values are *peak diversion under 100-yr operation*, not operational trigger thresholds. The rock-weir concept will need both a trigger (e.g., divert when Rock Creek discharge exceeds X cfs) and a maximum-divert cap; neither is set by Nord.

### 2.2 Conveyance to Corridor Heads

Diverted water is carried in a channelized reach until it reaches each corridor head, then released to spread and meander.

- **Concept geometry.** Channelized to corridor heads; thereafter overland spread per topography with limited supplemental grading (per concept draft).
- **Reference channel lengths from Nord:**
  - Sand Creek Alt. diversion channel: 7,440 ft (≈1.4 mi); excavation 813,640 cy (*Nord*, Table 6).
  - Tributary Alt. diversion channel: 3,620 ft (≈0.69 mi); excavation 188,938 cy (*Nord*, Table 7).
  - **Shallow bedrock is a significant cost driver** in both Nord alignments (*Nord*, Secs. 9.2.1.2, 9.2.2.2). Same constraint applies here.
- **Conveyance length to this concept's corridor heads.** `[VERIFY: DATA MISSING — depends on selected intake location and corridor-head locations.]`

### 2.3 Two Overland Corridors

- **North route.** Approximately **6 miles**, joining Sand Creek and re-entering Rock Creek near State Route 99 (per concept draft).
- **South route.** Approximately **4.4 miles**, returning to Rock Creek (per concept draft).
- **Combined treated linear length.** **~10.4 miles** of corridor.

### 2.4 Distributed Low Rock Weirs

Hundreds of low rock weirs (roughly 2–3 ft) along both corridors slow flow, spread it laterally, trap sediment, and promote infiltration.

- **Weir height range.** ~2–3 ft (per concept draft).
- **Weir count.** "Hundreds" total across the ~10.4 mi treated corridor (per concept draft); `[VERIFY: target count and per-mile density require a hydraulic-roughness target and per-weir backwater calculation.]`
- **Weir spacing.** `[VERIFY: DATA MISSING — typical low-tech process-based restoration spacing is based on the local hydraulic slope: weir crest of one should approximately align with the toe of the next at the design discharge. Set after corridor longitudinal profile is available.]`
- **Weir material and footprint.** `[VERIFY: DATA MISSING — assumed locally-sourced rock; per-weir footprint, anchoring, and apron design require field design.]`

**California DSOD jurisdictional context (applies to BOTH dams and weirs that impound water).**
> "Within the state of California, small dams are allowed to be constructed without state oversight if they fall below the jurisdictional size thresholds defined by the Division of Safety of Dams (DSOD). These thresholds allow unlimited volumes of storage if the dam height is below six feet."
> — *RockCreek_Reclamation_District-Infiltration_Feasibility_Study_Oct2023.md*, Sec. 8.0 (Conclusion)

A 2–3 ft rock weir is well below this 6-ft DSOD threshold and any meaningful storage threshold, so each individual structure should be non-jurisdictional. **Cumulative-impact and CDFW Sec. 1602 streambed alteration considerations are independent of DSOD and remain.**

### 2.5 Return to Rock Creek

- **North route return.** Sand Creek confluence with Rock Creek near SR 99.
- **South route return.** Rock Creek main channel (per concept draft).
- **Trade-off framing.** Concept seeks to trade **flood intensity for flood duration** while extracting a recharge and habitat dividend.

### 3.3 Bifurcation Flow Split

The Rock-Creek-to-Keefer-Slough flow split is **event-dependent and trending toward Keefer Slough** due to sediment deposition and vegetation growth at the bifurcation.

- **2011 FIS:** "approximately 44 percent of the 1-percent-annual-chance total Rock Creek discharge is diverted into Keefer Slough" (*Nord*, Sec. VII.B quoting FEMA 2011 FIS).
- **DWR 2D model (per Caltrans 2025):** 61% of Q100 now goes to Keefer Slough; only 39% remains in Rock Creek — driven by sediment deposition and vegetation at the bifurcation (*Flooding_Issues_Along_Hwy99_Mud_Creek_to_Rock_Creek.md*, Sec. 1).
- **Operational implication.** The bifurcation has shifted materially since the 2011 FIS analysis. Any flood-routing analysis must use the most recent flow-split rating (DWR 2D), not the FIS 44% split, while flagging that the new rating itself is still under FEMA review and not yet adopted (DWR-FEMA review expected to conclude 2027; *Flooding_Issues_Along_Hwy99_Mud_Creek_to_Rock_Creek.md*, "Butte County, DWR and FEMA").

### 3.4 Precipitation Basis

- **Nord HEC-HMS.** NOAA Atlas 14 point precipitation depths, areally reduced via Depth Areal Reduction Factors (DARF) from HMR59 (per recent CVHS practice). Design storm: 24-hour nested hypothetical event for each AEP, 10% through 0.2% (*Nord_Feasibility_Study_Part_A.md*, Sec. 6.1, Appendix 1).
- **Sand Creek Infiltration Study.** For long-term historical infiltration simulation, NOAA Atlas 14 was replaced with **hourly CRG gauge data, Feb 2000 – Aug 2023** (~24 years). CRG is ~17 mi northwest of Sand Creek but at similar elevation/climate. Average annual rainfall at CRG: 19.33 in; range 5.13–32.22 in (*RockCreek_Reclamation_District-Infiltration_Feasibility_Study_Oct2023.md*, Sec. 5.1, Tables 1–2).
- **Mean annual precipitation for the Rock Creek watershed:** 38 in (*Nord_PartB_2_Geomorphic_Assessment.md*, Physiographic Setting).

### 3.5 Hydraulic Models Available

- **DWR / Wood Rodgers HEC-RAS 2D model.** Covers Rock Creek + Keefer Slough + Sand Creek; not yet FEMA-approved; FEMA review expected to conclude 2027. *This is the most current and most relevant 2D model for any routing analysis of the concept.*
- **Nord HEC-RAS 6.1 model (built on DWR base).** Used for the Nord alternatives; 100 ft × 100 ft general grid, 10–50 ft cells on creeks/sloughs; 43 culverts modeled; USGS 2017 terrain (*Nord_Feasibility_Study_Part_A.md*, Sec. 6.2.1).
- **Sand Creek Infiltration Study HEC-RAS truncated model.** Same area, finer mesh on Sand Creek, used to develop rating curves of inundation area vs. inflow at the five candidate infiltration basins (*RockCreek_Reclamation_District-Infiltration_Feasibility_Study_Oct2023.md*, Sec. 6.1.2).
- **No streamflow gauge data exists for Rock Creek or its tributaries** (*Nord_Feasibility_Study_Part_A.md*, Sec. II.E). Models are uncalibrated to local high-flow events; calibration to adjacent gauges (Mud Creek, Little Chico Creek) is the available proxy.

## 4. Recharge-Feasibility Inputs (Subsurface)

The Sand Creek / Rock Creek Reclamation District Infiltration Feasibility Study is the **primary same-area source** for these inputs and should be treated as authoritative for the recharge screen.

### 4.1 Surface Infiltration Rate

- **GSA-determined long-term constant infiltration rate:** **0.7 ft/day (0.35 in/hr) maximum** at the five most-favorable Sand Creek basin sites (*RockCreek_Reclamation_District-Infiltration_Feasibility_Study_Oct2023.md*, Sec. 4.0 and Sec. 8.0).
- **NRCS-derived near-surface infiltration estimates** (depth-weighted harmonic mean Ksat, reduced 10× per GSA practice): most of the study area expected to support **less than 0.2 ft/day**; favorable channel-corridor zones up to **0.6 ft/day** (*Sand Creek Infiltration Study*, GSA Appendix Sec. 2.3).
- **Range for screening.** Use **0.2 ft/day** as the conservative (poor-soil) bound and **0.7 ft/day** as the optimistic (favorable-channel) bound.

### 4.2 Shallow Restrictive-Layer / Clay-Layer Reconciliation (Concept Draft's Open Question)

The concept draft asks for explicit reconciliation between the Sand Creek Infiltration Study findings and the prior Vina AEM/tTEM interpretation of a shallow clay layer impeding recharge. **Reconciliation per the source documents:**

- **Sand Creek Infiltration Study findings:** Hardpan / restrictive layers occur as **shallow as 2 ft below ground surface** at some Sand Creek well locations (WCR-114310, WCR-518070); most nearby wells show cemented material or rock within the top 10 ft (*Sand Creek Infiltration Study*, GSA Appendix Sec. 2.3).
- **Spatial variability is the key finding.** The five advanced infiltration sites (D1&2, E1, E2, J1, J2) were selected *because* they fall outside the shallow-restrictive-layer zones. Downstream Sand Creek (Redsluff gravelly loam) **lacks a restrictive layer in the top 80 inches**; upstream / northern Sand Creek and most non-channel areas have shallow restrictive layers (*Sand Creek Infiltration Study*, GSA Appendix Sec. 2.3, Figs. 3–4).
- **SAGBI suitability rating:** Downstream Sand Creek channel **good**; upper / northern tributary areas **poor to very poor** (*Sand Creek Infiltration Study*, GSA Appendix Sec. 2.3, Fig. 4).
- **Singer Creek confirms the same pattern in adjacent Tuscan soils:** Tuscan loams have a **duripan at 18–22 in bgs**; permeability of Tuscan soils is rated **slow to very slow** (*Singer_Creek_HDP_Vol_1.md*, Sec. 2.5).

**Implication for the rock-weir concept.** The shallow-clay-layer constraint is **real but spatially variable**. The northern corridor of this concept overlaps the Sand Creek watershed where favorable channel zones have been identified; some weir reaches will have favorable subsurface and some will not. The recharge benefit is therefore **not uniform along the corridor** — the corridor-specific recharge screen (Section 7 of the concept draft, and Deliverable 4) needs the same Frequency-Domain Electro-Magnetic (FDEM) survey approach Sand Creek used, supplemented by NRCS Web Soil Survey at the corridor scale.

### 4.3 Channel-Infiltration Bonus

> "Based on GSA's experience, increased channel infiltration can be as high as 50% of the total volume recharged in stormwater capture systems."
> — *RockCreek_Reclamation_District-Infiltration_Feasibility_Study_Oct2023.md*, GSA Appendix Sec. 4.0 (Recommendations)

This is **directly transferable and favorable** to the rock-weir concept. Distributed weirs (ponding plus along-channel infiltration) likely capture the same channel-infiltration premium the Sand Creek dams would.

### 4.4 Vadose-Zone Storage

- **Depth to groundwater:** 60–110 ft bgs (Oct 2022 SGMA data, Sand Creek site).
- **Groundwater elevation:** 130–160 ft above msl.
- **Permeable material extends:** ~300–350 ft bgs (Sec. 7.0 of Sand Creek Infiltration Study).
- **Tuscan aquifer parameters (Vina Subbasin, Lower Tuscan):**
  - Specific yield: 5.9–7.1%
  - Transmissivity: 2,322–23,650 sq ft/day
  - Horizontal hydraulic conductivity: 66–5,712 ft/day
  - *Source: Geosyntec Consultants 2021 Vina GSP via Sand Creek Infiltration Study Sec. 2.4.*
- **Implication.** Vadose-zone storage and aquifer transmissivity at the Sand Creek site are sufficient for recharge once water moves past the shallow restrictive layer; the constraint is at the surface, not deeper.

## 6. Key Assumptions Underlying This Concept

These are concept-stage assumptions that the screening analyses (Deliverable 5) and the next-steps work (Deliverable 4) must test, not adopt:

1. **The rock-weir mechanism is the right tool.** This concept assumes that distributed low-head grade control plus floodplain reactivation, applied at watershed scale (~10.4 mi of corridor), can produce a meaningful peak reduction. The peer evidence (see Deliverable 3) supports this for frequent-to-moderate events and explicitly cautions that attenuation diminishes at the rarest, largest events. The screening routing must demonstrate this — not assume it — particularly for Q100 and Q500.
2. **Diversion can be safely operated.** Concept assumes a controlled diversion that activates above a threshold and bypasses normal/low flows. Trigger and capacity are unset; both need a hydraulic screening.
3. **Corridor recharge feasibility is non-uniform but real.** Per Sand Creek Infiltration Study reconciliation in Sec. 4 of this document. Some weir reaches will recharge well; others, where shallow hardpan dominates, will primarily detain and evaporate. The recharge analysis must be reach-by-reach, not corridor-average.
4. **Land control is achievable through willing-landowner agreements / easements.** Both routes traverse private parcels (multiple family trusts). Easement strategy and a landowner inventory are explicit early-work items.
5. **No-rise can be demonstrated.** Because Rock Creek and Keefer Slough are within FEMA Special Flood Hazard Areas and will be in Zone AE after the DWR FIRM update (anticipated 2027), the concept must satisfy the FEMA no-rise requirement and pursue CLOMR / LOMR. See *Flooding_Issues_Along_Hwy99_Mud_Creek_to_Rock_Creek.md* for the regulatory framing.
6. **Habitat enhancement is the explicit objective.** Unlike Nord, this concept seeks to **enhance** habitat. The footprint is therefore expected to be net positive for vernal pool / riparian / wetland species *if* designed to avoid sensitive-species inundation and *if* it qualifies as restoration/creation/enhancement rather than impact. Singer Creek's NWP 27 pathway (CWA 404 nationwide permit for restoration) is the closest regional precedent (*Singer Creek HDP*, Sec. 1.3).
7. **Bifurcation maintenance is a separate but reinforcing project.** The Caltrans memo documents that sediment deposition and vegetation at the bifurcation have shifted the flow split from ~56/44 (RC/KS) to 39/61, materially increasing Keefer Slough flooding (*Flooding_Issues_Along_Hwy99...*, Sec. 1). Bifurcation maintenance reduces downstream Keefer flooding *independently* of upstream diversion; both could and probably should advance together.

## 7. What Is NOT Transferable from the Source Projects — Explicit Flags

| Source-project finding | Why NOT transferable to this concept |
| --- | --- |
| Nord's 7,990 ac-ft Sand Creek reservoir and the resulting Q100 attenuation from 8,010 → 6,020 cfs at SR 99 | This is dam-storage attenuation. A rock-weir corridor cannot match this storage; its attenuation comes from spreading and roughness, not impoundment. The numerical result does NOT carry over. |
| Nord's 46.4 ft dam at Sand Creek and 35.5 ft dam at the unnamed tributary | Dam-specific engineering, foundation requirements, DSOD jurisdiction, and reservoir-inundation environmental impacts are not relevant to 2–3 ft rock weirs. |
| Nord's 143 acres of environmental mitigation (~$286M) for the Sand Creek reservoir (the tributary alternative carries 82 ac / ~$164M) | The mitigation cost is driven by the reservoir-inundation footprint. A rock-weir corridor has a fundamentally different footprint and a different (and likely net-positive) impact / benefit profile. |
| Sand Creek Infiltration Study's per-basin recharge estimates (45.6 ac-ft baseline, up to 458 ac-ft at 100% dam height for Basin D1&2, etc.) | These are dam-pond-area infiltration estimates for specific impoundment basins, not rock-weir corridor estimates. The 0.7 ft/day rate transfers; the per-basin volumes do not. |
| Sand Creek Infiltration Study's finding that "very large flood events will quickly fill the basin early in the storm... and will pass the peak of such a flood event downstream relatively unattenuated" | Counterintuitively, this finding partly DOES apply to rock weirs in a literal sense — at extreme events, even distributed weirs see drowned hydraulics and reduced incremental attenuation. The mechanism differs (drowning rather than storage exhaustion), but the directional conclusion — diminishing returns at extremes — applies.
