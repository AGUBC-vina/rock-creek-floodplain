## Flow Split Issue at Bifurcation of Rock Creek

- Based on the proposed DWR 2D model result, the 100-yr flow discharge at the same location of the upstream boundary of the Caltrans 2D model is **2,390.90 cfs** at 1,099 feet upstream of the Garner Lane bridge. This value is **3.52 times** larger than the 100-yr flow discharge (680 cfs) of the original FEMA FIS report (refer to Figs. 1–4).
- The 100-yr flood discharge of Rock Creek from the upper watershed is **6,413 cfs**, which is divided at the Keefer Slough / Rock Creek bifurcation. The diverged flow discharge is:
  - **Rock Creek: 2,483 cfs (39%)**
  - **Keefer Slough: 3,867 cfs (61%)** — previous study by the Corps showed 44%
- The channel conveyance capacity of Rock Creek decreased from 56% to 39% due to sediment deposit and growing vegetation at Rock Creek.
- The channel conveyance capacity of Keefer Slough increased from 44% to 61% due to sediment deposit and growing vegetation at Rock Creek.
- Proper maintenance of the bifurcation area of Rock Creek is needed to recover the main role of the original Rock Creek, which conveys more flood water than Keefer Slough.

## Regular Maintenance Activity at the Bifurcation Area of Rock Creek

- There is no responsible agency or authority to achieve proper maintenance activity at the bifurcation area of Rock Creek.
- Regular maintenance at the bifurcation is urgently needed to recover the main role of the original flow conveyance capacity of Rock Creek. This maintenance activity before and after every year's flood season (October 15) will reduce the flood damage issues along SR 99.
- Regular maintenance activity at the bifurcation area of Rock Creek may be achieved by an MOU between Butte County, the private landowner, and other related agencies.

## Additional Overtopping Locations Along SR 99 from Mud Creek to Rock Creek

There are additional locations that overtop the highway or are subject to possible flooding at the edge of the traveled way along Highway 99 (SR 99) from north of Mud Creek to north of Rock Creek, excepting the current proposed project (refer to Figs. 5–14):

- **A.** Overtopping of Hwy 99 at North of Rock Creek Bridge
- **B.** Overtopping of Hwy 99 at North of Keefer Slough Bridge and Garden Brook Drive
- **C.** Overtopping of Hwy 99 at South of Keefer Slough Bridge and Fire Station 41 (current proposed project)
- **D.** Culvert Peak Flow and Edge of Traveled Way at North of Wilson Landing Drive
- **E.** Peak Flow at Edge of Traveled Way (South of Wilson Landing Drive)

There are many locations that need a proper flooding-reduction project to reduce the possible overtopping of the highway or flooding at the edge of the traveled way along SR 99 — by grade raising, installing additional culverts, and installing larger culverts.

The grade raising, additional culverts, and larger culverts outside of the proposed grade-raising project need further hydraulic study using 2-dimensional hydraulic models to obtain proper alternatives depending on the flooding condition of each location.

## Possible FEMA CLOMR or LOMR Submittal Process

- If the proposed project area (FEMA Zone A) is reassigned to FEMA Zone AE (Base Flood Elevation determined) on Rock Creek and Keefer Slough after 2027, a Conditional Letter of Map Revision (CLOMR) may ultimately be required for the future proposed project prior to construction.
- Current residents near Rock Creek and Keefer Slough would have to pay additional flood insurance due to the change of FEMA zoning from A to AE, because that additional area will be flooded more than the existing FEMA study area due to the increased flood discharge.
- The fundamental floodplain permitting requirement is that proposed actions in the floodway must not increase flood heights during the 100-year flood event — otherwise known as the "no-rise" requirement.
- The proposed project would require a CLOMR from FEMA commenting on whether the proposed project, if built as proposed, or proposed hydrology changes would meet minimum NFIP standards (see Title 44, Chapter 1, Code of Federal Regulations [CFR], Parts 60, 65, and 72) prior to starting the proposed project.
- After completing the proposed project, a Letter of Map Revision (LOMR) from FEMA would also be required, officially revising the current NFIP map to show changes to floodplains, regulatory floodways, or flood elevations (see 44 CFR Parts 60, 65, and 72).
- A timeline of 12 months is suggested for processing a CLOMR after the time of submittal. A post-construction LOMR is also required to modify the FEMA maps accordingly. Floodplain permits may be conditioned upon approval of a CLOMR and a post-construction LOMR.
- Both CLOMR and LOMR actions/approvals from FEMA are needed before and after construction of future proposed projects.

## Butte County, DWR and FEMA (Related Agencies)

- Based on communication with Butte County, the County has no jurisdiction for maintaining the bifurcation area of Rock Creek because it is property of private landowners. There was one cleanup activity at the bifurcation area in February 2025.
- DWR is currently working on an updated flood study and maps along the Rock Creek and Keefer Slough watershed area. DWR's 2D model result has not been approved by FEMA and Butte County yet. The updating of the FEMA FIS and FIRM map will be officially approved by FEMA in the near future. DWR expects the review and approval process will be completed by 2027.
- Based on the *Small Communities Flood Risk Reduction Program Feasibility Study for the Town of Nord, Final Report* (Butte County and DWR, by Wood Rodgers, Aug. 2023), there are various alternatives to reduce flood risk for the proposed area.
- The study identifies a preferred alternative of "Levees," which includes not only levees and floodwalls on Rock Creek and a portion of Sand Creek upstream of SR 99, but also the installation of a stabilization structure at the bifurcation of Rock Creek and Keefer Slough, a ring levee around the community of Nord, and agricultural flood easements and home elevation on the west side of SR 99.
- There is no fixed schedule or plan for the various alternatives to reduce flood risk for the proposed area and adjacent area at Rock Creek and Keefer Slough yet.
- Further communication and cooperation with other agencies such as DWR, FEMA, Caltrans, and Butte County may be needed to reduce the expected flood damage and related issues.

## Comments

- To recover the main role of the original flow conveyance capacity of Rock Creek, regular maintenance activity at the bifurcation area of Rock Creek is needed. Currently there is no responsible agency or authority to achieve proper maintenance at the bifurcation area of Rock Creek for reducing the flood damage along SR 99.
- Regular maintenance activity at the bifurcation area of Rock Creek may be achieved by an MOU between Butte County, the private landowner, and other related agencies.
- Additional proper flooding-reduction projects for the four overtopping/flooded locations along SR 99 are necessary to reduce the possible overtopping of the highway or flooding at the edge of the traveled way of SR 99.
- No fixed schedule or plan for the various alternatives to reduce the expected flood risk for the proposed and adjacent area at Rock Creek and Keefer Slough yet.
- Limitation of Caltrans' flood-reduction measures, because the main flood-related solutions or alternatives are outside of Caltrans' jurisdiction and right of way.
- Caltrans' grade-raise-with-additional-culverts project may not be necessary anymore if installation of a stabilization structure (such as a weir with gate) at the bifurcation of Rock Creek and Keefer Slough can control the diverged flood discharge of Keefer Slough.
- Depending on the DWR and FEMA study results after 2027, both CLOMR and LOMR approvals from FEMA may be needed before and after construction of proposed projects.
- Current residents near Rock Creek and Keefer Slough would have to pay additional flood insurance due to the change of FEMA zoning from A to AE.
- Further communication, discussion, and cooperation with other agencies such as DWR, FEMA, Caltrans, and Butte County is needed to reduce further flood damage and to find proper solutions to the flood-related issues.
